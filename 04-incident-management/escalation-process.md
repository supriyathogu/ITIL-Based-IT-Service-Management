# Incident Escalation Process

## Purpose
Escalation ensures that incidents receive the skills, authority, or attention required to progress.

## Functional escalation
A ticket is routed to a team or specialist with the appropriate technical expertise.

| Level | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|
| L1 – Service Desk | Initial contact, ticket logging, categorisation, basic approved checks |
| L2 – Technical Support | Deeper investigation within a specialist support area |
| L3 – Product / Engineering / Vendor | Complex defects, product-level investigation, or specialist intervention |

The exact support levels vary by organisation.

## Hierarchical escalation
An incident may be raised to a team lead, service owner, or management contact when business impact, risk, or service-level concerns require additional attention.

## Common escalation triggers
- The issue is outside the current team's permissions or skills.
- Multiple users or a critical service are affected.
- A target response or resolution time is at risk.
- The issue is recurring or has significant business impact.
- A security or safety concern requires specialist handling.

## Information to include when escalating
- Ticket key and concise summary.
- Affected users and services.
- Business impact and urgency assessment.
- Actions already taken and their results.
- Relevant error messages or evidence.
- Current workaround, if any.
- Specific assistance required.
